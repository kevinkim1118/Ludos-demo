Wireframe — Head-to-Head "Compare Two" flow — Desktop (1200px)
The Library's "compare two" path for deciding what to play. First you set an intent ("what are you in the mood for?"), which scopes the pool; then two sparse, name-forward cards face off with an OR badge — tap the one you prefer and the next pairing loads. The winner stays; the unchosen card is replaced by the next game in line. Opens as a modal over a dimmed Library. Grayscale lo-fi.
Annotations — how the compare flow works
1Modal over a dimmed Library. The flow is a focused aside, not a separate destination — Library stays visible behind the scrim so the user never loses their place. Closing returns them exactly where they were.
2Tap the card you prefer — the card is the choice (no buttons inside it). The winner stays put; the unchosen game is replaced by the next in line, so the running winner keeps facing fresh challengers until one game is left standing.
3Cards are deliberately sparse — cover, name, genre, time-to-finish, nothing else. The trust signals (match · fits-time · friends) were removed: head-to-head is a half-second gut pick, not a research task, and reading kills the speed Beli's pattern depends on.
4Undo / Skip — minimal controls. Undo steps back a pairing; Skip passes on this matchup. No scores or stars anywhere.
5The winner reveals as your pick, with a one-line "why it won." This is the payoff — a single confident answer pulled out of a stalled backlog.
6Short by design. The flow auto-stops the moment a game wins 3 in a row (or after a 5-compare cap), seeded best-match-first with the just-rejected hero pick excluded. The progress ticks up top show how short it is — no endless gauntlet.
7The chip = your chosen intent. Carried in from the intent screen ("Fast & fun"), it scopes the pool and is tappable to switch. It replaces a raw duration filter — intent already encompasses how much time you want to spend.
8Intent leads the flow. Before any pairing, "What are you in the mood for?" scopes the pool by genre + time-commitment (a fast loop vs. progress in a big game), so the cards face off on like terms. Orange tags show each option's internal filter (wireframe-only).
